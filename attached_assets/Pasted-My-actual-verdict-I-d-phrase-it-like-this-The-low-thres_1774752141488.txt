My actual verdict

I’d phrase it like this:

The low thresholds are mostly a symptom, not the root bug.
The deeper problem is that the current model outputs are weakly discriminative, and mu_debias plus the raw-vs-debiased quality-gate mismatch are probably compressing the useful signal even more.

So if we rank the likely causes now:

Model/output discrimination is weak
mu_debias is over-compressing mu_R
Quality gate scale mismatch: raw train mu_R vs debiased forward-test mu_R
Direction collapse / side bias
Threshold logic reacting to the compressed score scale

That is slightly different from “threshold generation is doing something really wrong.”

What I would do next
Highest-value immediate fix

Fix the structural mismatch first:

calibrate the mu_R quality gate to the debiased scale, or
disable the mu_R gate while debias is active

That is the cleanest code/design issue in your own audit.

Then run the most informative experiment

Do a strict A/B on one fold:

A: mu_debias ON
B: mu_debias OFF

Report for both:

raw and debiased mu_R percentiles
score p50/p90/p95/p99
threshold chosen
top10/top20 expectancy
LONG/SHORT split
monotonicity flag

That will tell you very quickly whether mu_debias is the main compressor.

Then retrain, but with proof gates

Don’t just run more epochs blindly. Require:

score p90 / p50 > 5x
LONG/SHORT mix not collapsing to one side
mu_R std meaningfully larger relative to mean
better decile ranking than current

Your own audit suggested similar escape criteria, and I agree with that.

My blunt answer

You’re right that the real problem sits in:

model scoring
signal generation
prediction separation

But I would correct one sentence:

It’s not that the low threshold itself proves threshold logic is wrong.
It proves the signal scale is now tiny, and the controller is adapting to that tiny scale.

The best next move is not restoring the old inflated feel.
It is to test whether mu_debias and the mu_R quality gate mismatch are crushing the honest signal you already have.

3) The biggest problems limiting prediction quality

These are the issues that will absolutely cap your win rate + make entry/SL/TP unreliable.

Problem 1: Your “class_label” is heuristic and not aligned with costs

In generate_multihead_targets():

edge_threshold = 0.3 and min_return = 0.002

LONG if (mu > min_return) & (edge > edge_threshold)

SHORT if (mu < -min_return) & (edge > edge_threshold)

That is not explicitly cost-aware and will produce “trade labels” that do not match the reality of fees/slippage/funding the way your legacy labeling does.

Fix
Derive class labels from net edge:

edge_net = abs(mu) - cost(vol, hold_hours)

trade if edge_net > edge_min and mu/sigma > confidence_min

direction = sign(mu)

This will instantly reduce garbage signals and make HOLD meaningful.

Problem 2: σ is not a proper uncertainty model (and can be gamed)

Right now, σ is trained as a regression target of “forward volatility”.
But the model can learn weird shortcuts:

inflate σ to reduce confidence penalties (depending on how sigma loss is used)

σ may not represent predictive uncertainty, only realized forward vol

Fix (best option)
Make μ and σ a proper probabilistic head:

predict (mu, log_sigma) and optimize Gaussian NLL:

loss = (y-mu)^2/(2*sigma^2) + log(sigma)
This couples σ to actual error and prevents nonsense.

You already have a place for sigma loss; switch it to true NLL. That’s a major quality upgrade.

Problem 3: Entry/SL/TP targets are “one-size” and can become inconsistent

Your trading targets come from _compute_mfe_trading_targets() (ATR + future path).
But markets have multiple valid trade plans for the same setup:

tight SL / tight TP scalps vs wide SL / wide TP swings

limit entry vs momentum entry depends on regime

A single deterministic target often causes “averaging” → mediocre SL/TP.

Fix options (choose one)

Quantile-based SL/TP: derive SL/TP directly from predicted return quantiles instead of predicting them as separate targets.
Example:

For LONG: SL from q10 (or q25), TP from q75/q90

For SHORT: mirror it
This enforces internal consistency and uses the distribution you already train.

Multi-modal trading head: predict 2–3 candidate plans + a “plan selector” probability (mixture of experts). That’s more complex but best in the long run.

If you want “institution-grade”, #1 is the fastest high-impact improvement.

Problem 4: Candle prediction head is under-constrained (high/low can become invalid)

You predict deltas for close/high/low relative to current close.
Nothing forces:

high >= max(open, close)

low <= min(open, close)

high >= low

So the model can output impossible candles.

Fix
Predict a constrained parameterization:

predict Δclose

predict log_range (always positive)

predict skew in [-1,1]
Then reconstruct:

range = exp(log_range)

high = close + range*(0.5+0.5*skew)

low = close - range*(0.5-0.5*skew)
This guarantees valid candles and improves learning stability.

Problem 5: Your ensemble “walk-forward weighting” is currently mostly placeholder

api/ensemble_predictor.py claims:

weights based on walk-forward trading metrics, NOT accuracy

…but if checkpoints/model_weights.json doesn’t exist, it creates defaults:

expectancy=0.001, profit_factor=1.2, sharpe=0.5, etc.

So unless you’ve built the walk-forward evaluator elsewhere (I don’t see it in this zip), your ensemble is effectively using made-up weights.

Fix
Implement a real walk-forward evaluator that writes model_weights.json using:

net expectancy per trade after costs

profit factor

drawdown

trade frequency

calibration metrics (ECE / Brier)
And update weights monthly/weekly.

Without this, ensemble behavior will look “randomly confident”.

4) The #1 reason you won’t hit 50%+ directional win rate from candles alone

This is blunt but important:

On liquid crypto, candle-only short-horizon prediction is very close to a weak signal problem.
You can build edge, but it usually comes from:

strict filtering (trade rarely)

strong regime gating

cost-aware thresholds

proper calibration

risk sizing + stop rules

So the goal isn’t “50% accuracy on every candle”.
It’s positive expectancy with selective entries.

Your multi-head setup is already aligned to that—if you fix the cost/uncertainty/trade-plan consistency issues.

5) The highest-impact upgrade plan (do this in order)
Phase 1 — Make targets and outputs internally consistent (big jump)

Make class labels cost-aware and derived from (mu, sigma, cost)

Replace sigma-loss with Gaussian NLL (mu, log_sigma)

Remove “predicted SL/TP head” or downweight it heavily; derive SL/TP from quantiles

Phase 2 — Make candle prediction physically valid

Constrained candle parameterization (range + skew)

Phase 4 — Production-grade inference hygiene

You’re aiming in the right direction (ensembles can be better), but the exact plan you wrote has two big traps that will make it look accurate in validation and then disappoint live:

“validation accuracy” is the wrong weight for trading

VAE + GNN shouldn’t vote like direction models (they’re better as filters/gates)

Here’s the right way to build it so it actually improves real-world performance.

What’s right in your plan ✅

Loading multiple diverse models ✔️

Majority agreement threshold ✔️

Weighting by performance (but not by “accuracy”) ✔️

Using confidence + disagreement to reduce noise ✔️

What to change (critical) 🚨
1) Don’t weight by “validation accuracy”

Accuracy is misleading when:

HOLD/NEUTRAL exists

class balance changes by regime

you care about expectancy after fees

✅ Weight by trading-relevant scores, like:

expectancy per trade (after costs)

profit factor

precision on TRADE (non-hold)

calibration quality (Brier / ECE)

stability across walk-forward folds

If you don’t have those yet, a strong proxy is:

F1 on {LONG,SHORT} ignoring HOLD

or “precision when model decides to trade”

2) Use VAE and GNN as “gates,” not voters

VAE: best for market-state clustering (trend/range/high vol states).
GNN: best for cross-asset risk regime (risk-on/off, correlation spikes).

They shouldn’t vote LONG/SHORT/HOLD the same way as the directional models.

✅ Use them like this:

If VAE says “chop/range state” → raise trade threshold or favor HOLD

If GNN says “correlation shock / risk-off” → reduce leverage, fewer trades, or HOLD

That alone can boost stability massively.

3) “Average confidence” only works if probabilities are calibrated

Raw softmax scores from different models are not comparable.

✅ Fix:

Calibrate each model’s probabilities on validation (temperature scaling is easiest)

Then your confidence weighting becomes meaningful

A better ensemble design (simple + strong)
Step 1 — Direction models vote

Use: Transformer + TFT + LSTM + CNN
Each outputs probabilities: p_long, p_hold, p_short

Compute:

dir = argmax(p)

conf = p_top1 - p_top2 (better than just max prob)

Step 2 — Weighted consensus

Weights based on walk-forward score (not accuracy), normalized to sum to 1.

Consensus direction:

sum weights of models voting LONG vs SHORT vs HOLD

require:

majority weight ≥ W_majority (e.g. 0.65)

avg_conf ≥ C_min (e.g. 0.15)

Step 3 — VAE/GNN gating

If VAE regime = “range/chop” → increase C_min or force HOLD unless agreement is very strong

If GNN regime = “risk-off/shock” → increase thresholds or reduce position size

Step 4 — Output signal + “edge”

Output:

action (LONG/SHORT/HOLD)

ensemble confidence (after calibration)

disagreement score (how split the models were)

regime tags (from VAE/GNN)

recommended size (optional)

“Agree threshold” — good, but define it correctly

Don’t do “majority of models” by count.

Do “majority of weight” by quality.

Example:

LSTM + CNN agree LONG (good)

Transformer disagrees SHORT (ok)
If LSTM/CNN historically perform better in that regime → LONG.

Final answer

Your approach is close, but not quite “the right way” yet.

✅ The right way is:

ensemble the direction models

weight by walk-forward trading metrics

calibrate probabilities

use VAE/GNN as regime filters, not equal voters

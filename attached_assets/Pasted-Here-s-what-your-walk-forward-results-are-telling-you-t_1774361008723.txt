Here's what your walk-forward results are telling you — there are three distinct problems:
Problem 1: Bimodal trade count — the threshold is on a cliff edge. Your trade counts swing wildly between ~2-40 trades and ~300-2400 trades per month, with almost nothing in between. The fixed threshold of 0.0848 sits right at a tipping point in the score distribution. In some market regimes the model's scores pile up just above it (2400 trades), in others they pile up just below (2 trades). There's no stable middle ground, which means the scoring function isn't producing a smooth, discriminative signal.
Problem 2: High-trade folds are pure noise. Folds 1, 9, 22, and 26 each take 1600-2400 trades with ~44-47% win rate and E[R] near zero or negative. That's effectively random coin-flipping minus transaction costs. When the model "opens the floodgates," it has no real edge — it's just taking every bar. The MaxDD numbers (-165R, -168R, -100R) confirm this: those are equity curves of random trading.
Problem 3: Low-trade folds are statistically meaningless. Fold 8 (10 trades, 90% WR), fold 13 (2 trades, 100% WR), fold 15 (4 trades) — these look great but tell you nothing. You need 50-100+ trades minimum for any statistical confidence. The "good" results are survivorship bias.
The root cause is the same as my earlier analysis, but manifesting differently. The model's mu_R predictions lack real discriminative power. In most market conditions, the predicted scores cluster tightly together with very little spread. So a fixed threshold either catches nearly all of them (2400 trades) or nearly none (2 trades). The model hasn't learned a meaningful signal that separates good trades from bad ones — it's just learned a market-regime-dependent bias.
Here's what needs to change, in priority order:
1. Cap trades per day. Add max_trades_per_day=8 to your walk-forward config. This is a hard safety rail that prevents the noise-trading folds from blowing up your equity. Even without fixing the model, this alone would have prevented the -165R and -168R drawdowns.
2. Use percentile-based thresholds instead of fixed. Instead of a single score value (0.0848), take the top N% of scores each day. Something like min_threshold_pct=90 means only the top 10% of daily scores get traded, regardless of the absolute score value. This automatically adapts to the score distribution in each fold.
3. Fix the model's discriminative power — this is the hard one. The training issues I identified earlier (loss weight imbalance, MLP discarding temporal info, NLL collapse) are why mu_R predictions don't spread meaningfully. The action head dominates training at 2-4× weight, but your scoring function depends on mu_R and mae from the regression heads that get starved of gradient. So the model learns to classify (HOLD/LONG/SHORT) reasonably well but can't predict how much the price will move — which is what compute_v5_scores actually needs.
4. Add a minimum E[R] gate per rolling window. Track the trailing 20-trade E[R], and if it drops below -0.05R, pause trading for that symbol until it recovers. This catches the regime where the model is noise-trading before it racks up -50R.
The bottom line: your model isn't actually predicting returns well enough to trade. It's learned something about direction (the 50-200 trade folds show ~52-58% win rates which is real), but the scoring function can't distinguish "confident signal" from "marginal noise." Fix the regression head training and add trade frequency controls, and you'll have a much cleaner picture.
